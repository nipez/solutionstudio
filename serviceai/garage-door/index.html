Emergency calls go unanswered

A broken spring or a door stuck open does not wait for business hours — and the homeowner calls down the list until someone answers. I build after-hours answering, overflow coverage, missed-call recovery, urgency triage, appointment booking, and human escalation around your actual rules.

You win the emergency call instead of the next company on the list.

Marketing spend you cannot trace to jobs

Ads, Local Services, the website, and referrals all generate leads — but few owners can say which dollar produced which booked job, or how fast leads get a response. I connect lead sources, call answering, follow-up sequences, and booking into one tracked pipeline.

You know what each lead source actually earns — and no lead goes cold.

Customer history scattered across systems

The door installed nine years ago, the spring replaced twice, the opener model, the gate code — spread across QuickBooks, spreadsheets, and someone's memory. I build the CRM and customer-history layer so every call starts with the full picture of the property and its equipment.

Repeat customers are recognized — and replacement opportunities surface on time.

Door and opener quotes wait too long

New doors mean measurements, panel styles, insulation, springs, openers, and options — and quotes that sit in a truck or an inbox. I connect job details, pricing logic, materials, options, customer approvals, and follow-up into a faster estimating and proposal workflow.

Customers choose their door while they are still ready to buy.

Repairs and installs fight for the schedule

Same-day repairs, scheduled installs, parts availability, and technician skill all collide in one calendar that usually lives in one person's head. I turn those constraints into a usable scheduling, technician-matching, job-brief, and office-to-field workflow.

Less daily juggling and better-prepared technicians.

Owners see the numbers too late

Repair versus install margin, callback rates, opener and service-plan renewals, and job costs usually surface after the month closes. I connect calls, bookings, jobs, estimated versus actual costs, and margin into a current operating view.

Problems surface while there is still time to act.

Let's Build Together

A few straight answers.

Do I need to replace my current field-service software?

We review your current systems and software platforms together and decide what still makes sense to keep and build on — and what is better replaced with a more custom, flexible solution. The right mix is often simpler and more cost-effective over time than forcing workarounds onto tools that no longer fit.

Can an AI agent really handle a broken-spring emergency call?

Yes, when the calendar, business rules, escalation paths, and integrations support it. An emergency call should be recognized as one, booked against real availability, and handed to a person the moment the system is unsure.

Will this replace my office staff or technicians?

The goal is to remove repetitive work and broken handoffs, not replace the judgment and relationships that make the company successful.

Can you work with QuickBooks and our existing CRM or field-service software?

Often, but integration feasibility depends on the specific system, version, API, export options, and data quality. I confirm that before promising a build.

Can you help with marketing too?

Yes — marketing is central to the work. Solution Studio is led by a lifelong marketer and takes an AI approach to demand generation, campaigns, positioning, lead response, follow-up, and conversion systems — so you generate more of the right demand and can see which marketing produces booked jobs.

How are projects scoped and priced?

The first engagement is scoped around one operating problem. A Service Operations Review can be fixed-scope; a pilot build is priced after the workflow, data, integration, and approval requirements are clear. I do not recommend a large build before the first problem is understood.
