01 The problem

Everyone's telling you to “use AI.” Nobody's helping you do it on a nonprofit budget.

“I'm writing grant reports until midnight. I don't have time to figure out ChatGPT.”

Small teams are stretched thin running mission-critical programs while drowning in manual reporting, disconnected systems, and admin work. Funders want more data every year. Enterprise software is priced for orgs ten times your size. And the AI consulting market starts at $20k.

  • Enterprise software priced for someone else

    You don't need Raiser's Edge for a 4-person team. You need the three features you actually use, at a tenth of the price.

  • Reporting eats program time

    Hours every week pulling numbers from four systems, formatting them for funders, copy-pasting the same narrative in different word counts.

  • Systems that don't talk to each other

    CRM, accounting, program tracker, volunteer DB, email platform. Every report is a manual reconciliation.

  • AI advice is either $20k or a YouTube video

    Big-firm consulting is out of reach. Self-serve courses sit unfinished. There's no middle path built for how nonprofits actually buy.

Solution Studio has been quietly building for small teams since 2012. Nonprofits are the ones doing the most with the least — and the ones AI can help most.

Over the years we've watched a lot of “next big things” come and go. Websites, then social, then funnels, then no-code, then crypto. Most were noise. AI isn't.

But the conversation around it has gotten unbearable. Every consultant pitches a $20k engagement. Every vendor promises a platform. Every funder asks why you're not "leveraging AI" without offering to pay for any of it.

Meanwhile actual nonprofit teams are exhausted, a little behind on grant reports, and not sure who to ask. They don't need a transformation. They need someone who'll spend two hours a month with them and quietly hand back the time they keep losing to admin.

That's what this is. A real person who knows the tools, builds the small things, works within nonprofit budgets — and tells you the truth, including when AI isn't the answer.
